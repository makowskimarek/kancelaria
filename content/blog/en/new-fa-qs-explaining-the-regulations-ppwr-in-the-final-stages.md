## FAQ, or ‘please don’t worry, we’re still working out the details’

The new FAQ is intended to provide clarification. The document itself states that it has been prepared for the European Commission, reflects the views of its authors, and is intended to assist businesses, national authorities and citizens in applying the PPWR. It is therefore neither an amendment to the Regulation nor an implementing act. In practice, however, it will serve as one of the key reference points for businesses when determining how the Commission interprets the new obligations.

This is already the second version of the document. The FAQ indicates that it contains new and updated information compared to the first version from March 2026, and the changes are marked as ‘NEW’ or ‘UPDATED’. The very fact that such significant clarifications are appearing just a few days before the regulations come into force clearly illustrates the scale of the regulatory confusion. The PPWR is a very broad piece of legislation, covering essentially all packaging placed on the EU market, regardless of the material, country of manufacture and whether the packaging is empty or already contains a product.

## When does the PPWR come into force?

The PPWR entered into force on 11 February 2025, but its general date of application is 12 August 2026. At the same time, some obligations will apply at a later date, including those relating to recyclability, recycled content, bans on certain types of packaging and reuse targets.

This phased approach is important, but it should not lull us into a false sense of security. From 12 August 2026, significant obligations will come into force, including those relating to restrictions on substances of concern, specific requirements for producers, documentation, declarations of conformity and market surveillance. In its FAQs, the Commission confirms, amongst other things, that the obligation to minimise the content of substances of concern in packaging will apply from 12 August 2026, and that the existing EN 13428:2004 standard may be used until the standards are updated.

## What about packaging already in stock?

One of the most practical clarifications concerns packaging manufactured before 12 August 2026 but not yet placed on the market by that date. The Commission explains that such packaging does not need to be destroyed, reprocessed or relabelled. With regard to the requirements for unique identification and manufacturer details, it is possible to use an accompanying document. This is a sensible clarification – it is just a pity that it has come at a time when some businesses have probably already experienced a minor logistical and labelling crisis.

The FAQ also states that packaging placed on the market before 12 August 2026 may remain on that market, even if it does not comply with the PPWR. This is important information for manufacturers, importers, distributors and retail chains, who need to distinguish between packaging already placed on the market and packaging that has merely been manufactured or is in stock.

## Those with the right documents can rest easy

The new FAQs place a strong emphasis on documentation. Suppliers of packaging or packaging materials are generally not responsible for drawing up a declaration of conformity, but they must provide the manufacturer with all the information and documents needed to demonstrate the packaging’s compliance with the PPWR. The Commission explicitly states that suppliers may not refuse to provide such documentation.

At the same time, responsibility for the packaging’s compliance rests with the manufacturer – that is, the entity placing the packaging or the product in its packaging on the EU market – which, in practice, is most often the manufacturer or importer. In other words: you can ask a supplier for documents, you can engage a laboratory, you can use certification, but you cannot ‘delegate’ the legal responsibility for the packaging’s compliance in such a way that it ceases to lie with the company. This is not a ‘pass it on and forget it’ type of obligation.

The FAQ clearly states that the obligation to prepare technical documentation cannot be delegated. The manufacturer may seek support from other entities, but remains the operator responsible for the compliance of the packaging placed on the market. Furthermore, this responsibility cannot be effectively transferred by a contract alone. A contract with a supplier is still necessary, but it cannot replace a compliance system.

This is significant from the perspective of current market practice, which is currently inconsistent. Many entities that are, in reality, manufacturers under the PPWR seek either to delegate their obligations to other entities or even to assign this role to entities at earlier stages of the supply chain. This remains legally ineffective, and it remains an open question to what extent this stems from deliberate action on the part of these entities, and to what extent it results from the scale of the information chaos surrounding the PPWR.

## Does every lid have to be given its own number?

The Commission has also clarified the issue of packaging traceability. Article 15(5) of the PPWR requires that packaging bear information enabling its unique identification, e.g. a serial number, batch number or other equivalent identifier. The aim is to enable the packaging to be linked to the technical documentation and the declaration of conformity.

Fortunately, the Commission does not expect every single component of the packaging to be marked separately. In the case of a yoghurt pot consisting of a pot, a lid and a label, it may be sufficient to place the required information on a single component of the sales packaging. As a general rule, the identification may refer to the type of packaging or the production batch, rather than to each individual item.

This is important because, without this clarification, some businesses might conclude that, from 12 August, the world of packaging should turn into a system for tracking individual atoms. That is not the point – but the point remains that the packaging must be linkable to the relevant documentation.

## Declaration of Conformity – one package, many questions

The FAQ also clarifies the rules on conformity assessment and the declaration of conformity. As a general rule, unless a particular regulation provides for a later date of application, businesses should carry out conformity assessments from 12 August 2026.

The Commission states that the conformity assessment and declaration of conformity should be drawn up for the entire packaging unit. For a bottle with a cap and a label, a single assessment and a single declaration for the entire unit may therefore suffice, provided that the documentation includes information on all components.

A linguistic clarification is also important. The declaration of conformity must be drawn up in the languages required by the Member State to which the packaged products are supplied, so that the market surveillance authority can verify it upon request. When operating across multiple EU countries, the language issue ceases to be a mere administrative formality and becomes a practical consideration in implementation.

## Will goods disappear from the market from 12 August?

The Commission provides a reassuring answer to this question. The FAQs state that the enforcement of obligations applicable from 12 August 2026 should not disrupt trade flows, supply chains or consumers’ access to goods. As a first step, the authority should call upon the relevant entity to remedy the non-compliance and give it the opportunity to take corrective action. Only a persistent failure to rectify the non-compliance may lead to further measures, such as banning, withdrawing or recalling non-compliant packaging.

This is an important section of the FAQs, but it should not be interpreted as an exemption from obligations. Rather, the Commission is signalling that supervisory authorities should, initially, support businesses, provide information and set reasonable deadlines for compliance. This does not, however, mean that documentation can remain merely on paper, or that packaging compliance can remain a mere aspiration.

## EPR, micro-enterprises and a false sense of security

The FAQ also contains an important clarification regarding extended producer responsibility (known as EPR). The Commission points out that the PPWR does not provide for a general exemption from EPR obligations for micro-enterprises. All producers are responsible for the management of packaging waste which they place on the market for the first time within the territory of a given Member State, including registration and reporting.

The PPWR provides for certain reporting simplifications for smaller producers, in particular those placing less than 10 tonnes of packaging per year on the market of a given Member State, but this is not a general loophole allowing them to opt out of the system. This is yet another example of how the PPWR requires a precise definition of a business’s role, rather than a simple question: ‘Are we large or small?’.

## What should businesses do now?

Firstly, it is necessary to determine what role the business plays: producer, importer, distributor, packaging supplier, producer of packaged products, responsible party under the EPR system, or several of these roles simultaneously. Under the PPWR, these roles can appear to overlap, and the reality of business rarely fits neatly into a single, neat column in an Excel spreadsheet.

Next, the packaging documentation must be reviewed: declarations of conformity, technical documentation, material data, information from suppliers, packaging identification, labelling, confirmations regarding substances of concern, PFAS, heavy metals, recyclability, recycled content and requirements specific to the particular type of packaging.

It is also important to remember to update contracts with suppliers. Since the supplier is required to provide the information needed to demonstrate compliance, it is worth explicitly setting out the scope of this information, deadlines, liability for any inaccuracies, the obligation to update documents, and support in the event of an inspection.

Finally, it is worth drawing up internal guidelines for the PPWR document workflow. Who collects the documents? Who assesses their completeness? Who signs the declaration? Who makes the necessary corrections to the packaging? Who is responsible for translations? Who checks the stock packaging? Who liaises with the supplier? When it comes to the PPWR, the absence of a procedure can be more costly than the procedure itself.

## PPWR – a regulation that cannot be implemented by simply taking it in your stride

The European Commission’s new FAQ was much needed and, in many respects, sensibly alleviates practical concerns. The problem is that it has come very late, and we have the impression that it was introduced on the sly, at a stage when the lion’s share of the market was already at an advanced stage of implementing changes that now require revision. For businesses, this means they must quickly review their existing implementation plans and check whether certain decisions taken previously require adjustment.

The PPWR is one of those pieces of legislation that does not stop at the legal department. It affects procurement, quality, packaging technology, marketing, sales, logistics, imports, technical documentation, EPR and supplier relations. And given that the Commission is publishing key clarifications less than two weeks before the regulations come into force, businesses should all the more ensure they have their own structured system for monitoring and implementing the changes.

In our practice, we support businesses in analysing their obligations under the PPWR, defining roles within the supply chain, reviewing packaging documentation, preparing declarations and compliance procedures, updating contracts with suppliers, and assessing risks associated with product packaging. The Commission’s new FAQs provide a good opportunity to check whether the implementation of the PPWR within an organisation actually aligns with the current guidance – rather than simply the version of the regulations that someone was trying to understand a few months ago.
