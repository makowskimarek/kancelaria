## EUDR – we are entering the practical implementation phase

The EUDR, or Regulation 2023/1115 on the placing on the EU market and export from the Union of certain goods and products linked to deforestation and forest degradation, is intended to ensure that specific goods placed on the EU market, placed on the EU market or exported from the EU do not contribute to deforestation or forest degradation. The regulation covers seven main commodities: cattle, cocoa, coffee, palm oil, rubber, soya and timber, as well as selected derived products.

Following an earlier postponement of the implementation dates, the EUDR is to apply from 30 December 2026 to large and medium-sized operators, as well as micro and small operators previously covered by the EU Timber Regulation, and from 30 June 2027 to the remaining micro and small operators.

The new documents do not appear to be what the industry was expecting following the delay to the EUDR at the end of last year and the substantial announcements regarding a significant reduction in the complexity of procedures. At the same time, they are significant because they show that preparations for the implementation of the EUDR have entered the operational phase and that no further delay to its entry into force is to be expected. It is no longer just a matter of general due diligence obligations, but of specific questions: which products will ultimately fall within the scope of the regulation, how will DDS declarations be submitted, when will it be possible to use simplified declarations, and how is the information system intended to operate?

## Delegated Act – changes to the scope of products covered by the EUDR

The first document is a delegated act: Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) …/… amending Regulation (EU) 2023/1115 as regards the list of relevant commodities and relevant products, designated as **C/2026/4920 final**, which can be viewed (currently available in English only) [at this link](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=intcom:Ares%282025%293099313). This act amends Annex I to the EUDR, i.e. the list of products covered by the Regulation.

According to the Commission’s press release, the delegated act updates and simplifies the scope of products. Among other things, the following are to be removed from the scope of the EUDR: hides and tanned hides of bovine animals, retreaded tyres, soya for sowing, vulcanised rubber articles, conveyor and drive belts, and aircraft and car seats. At the same time, according to the draft published on the European Commission’s website, the scope is to be expanded to include, amongst other things, instant coffee, certain palm oil derivatives and frozen beef tongues.

It is also important to note that the new products added to the scope of the EUDR are not due to be covered by the regulation until 30 December 2027. This is intended to give businesses time to prepare their supply chains, collect data and adapt their procedures.

The delegated act does not alter the list of core commodities covered by the EUDR. These remain cattle, cocoa, coffee, palm oil, rubber, soya and timber. The changes, however, concern derivative products, i.e. specific codes and categories listed in Annex I.

It is important to note, however, that the delegated act has been adopted by the Commission and forwarded to the European Parliament and the Council for scrutiny. This means that, before the changes can be applied in practice, their final publication status and entry into force must be monitored.

## Implementing Act – technical simplifications to the EUDR system

The second document is Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1565 of 13 July 2026, published in the Official Journal of the EU on 14 July 2026, available in Polish [at this link](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/PL/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32026R1565). This act amends Implementing Regulation 2024/3084 on the operation of the EUDR information system.

This document has practical significance. The EUDR information system is intended for the submission of due diligence statements (DDS) and simplified declarations. The amendment introduces measures to facilitate the use of the system, including simplified declarations for micro and small primary operators, contingency measures and other measures to simplify the use of the system.

The Commission has also highlighted an update to the technical specifications for the APIs and announced further system functionalities. This is important information for businesses planning to integrate their ERP systems, compliance tools or supplier management systems with the EUDR system.

## What should businesses do?

The new documents do not relieve businesses of their obligation to prepare for the EUDR. On the contrary, these changes should be seen as a definitive signal that the implementation of the EUDR will go ahead without further delay, albeit in a simplified form.

Firstly, companies should re-examine whether their products fall within the scope of Annex I to the EUDR, taking into account the changes resulting from the delegated act. This applies in particular to entities operating in the food, packaging, timber, rubber, retail, logistics and e-commerce sectors.

Secondly, the company’s role in the supply chain must be determined. Different obligations will apply to the operator, the trader, the importer, the exporter, the manufacturer using a given raw material, or an entity operating at a later stage in the supply chain.

Thirdly, it is essential to prepare the organisation to work with the EUDR information system. This involves not only the technical submission of DDSs, but also data collection, verification of reference numbers, archiving of documentation, assignment of internal responsibilities and preparation of contingency procedures.

Fourthly, it is time to review contracts with suppliers, using the period of negotiating terms to clarify EUDR-related issues in mutual relations. The EUDR will require the collection of data, documents and assurances from business partners. Without appropriate contractual clauses, a business may find it difficult to demonstrate due diligence or to respond swiftly to gaps in documentation.

## The EUDR requires ongoing monitoring, not a one-off analysis

The publication of new documents shows that the EUDR is not a static regulation. The scope of products may be clarified, the information system will be developed, and the practices of authorities and market participants will only take shape once the regulations come into force. Recent years have demonstrated the active involvement of businesses in shaping the law, so further amendments are to be expected as the regulations come into force and are ‘put to the test’ in the business world.

Therefore, preparation for the EUDR should not be limited to a one-off check to see whether a product is listed in Annex I. It is essential to monitor changes on an ongoing basis, update procedures, verify documents from suppliers and adapt internal systems to the subsequent stages of implementation.
